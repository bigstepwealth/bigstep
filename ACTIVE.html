The BIG STEP approach

Six Phases

The framework separates the decisions you make before investing from the actions you may take as markets change. The first priority is a suitable long-term allocation; Bubble and Crisis Plans are pre-defined responses for less frequent market conditions.

A · Allocation

Four-Box Investment Solution

Before selecting funds, decide what each part of your money is meant to do. The framework presents the Short-Term Box and Growth Box together as the core portfolio, while the Safety Box protects the household and the Extra Risk-Return Box is optional.

Protect

1. Safety Box

Includes: Emergency fund, life insurance and health insurance.

Keep emergency money accessible and separate from money exposed to market volatility. Review insurance cover for family responsibilities and liabilities.

Near-term needs

2. Short-Term Box

Includes: Money needed in under five years, using suitable debt funds, banking/FD options or equity savings where appropriate.

The shorter the time until spending, the more important liquidity and avoiding a forced sale become.

Core portfolio

3. Growth Box

Includes: Strategic asset allocation, the A.C.T.I.V.E. Framework, Hi-Five equity strategy, Core–Alpha debt strategy and entry/exit rules.

This is the primary long-term wealth-building portfolio and is reviewed and rebalanced systematically.

Optional satellite

4. Extra Risk-Return Box

Includes: High Risk funds- Thematic / Sectoral.

This box is for suitable investors only. Keep higher-risk or concentrated ideas separate from essential goal money and size them deliberately.

Practical order: Establish safety reserves and insurance first; identify near-term liabilities; then set the Growth Box allocation. Consider the Extra Risk-Return Box only if the core plan and risk capacity support it.

C · Cycle Awareness

Bubble Market Plan

The objective is not to predict the exact market peak. Pre-decide a portion of your Equity allocation to be deployed into Dynamic Asset Allocation Funds if there are signs of Bubble.

📊
ValuationsUnusually high versus their own history
↗
Price vs. earningsPrices running far ahead of earnings
⚡
Investor behaviourSpeculation and exuberance becoming excessive
◔
Market breadthFewer shares participating; breadth weakening

Require multiple independent signals. A high valuation alone does not prove a bubble, and a bubble can persist longer than expected.

TRIGGER 1 · Bubble WarningFirst signal cluster

Several independent indicators show stretched valuations and excessive market behaviour.

50%Move half of the pre-designated Bubble Plan amount (B) from its equity sleeve into a selected Dynamic Asset Allocation Fund (DAAF).
TRIGGER 2 · Elevated Bubble RiskConfirmation required

The warning persists or intensifies, with further confirmation from valuations, speculation or market breadth.

50%Move the remaining half of B into the selected DAAF. Do not trigger solely because prices rise again.
DAAFs are market-linked and are not capital-guaranteed. These are proposed operating rules, not historically validated signals; document investor-specific thresholds in advance.

V · Value Realisation

Rebalance to Stay the Course—not “Set and Forget”

Market movements change the portfolio weights even if you do nothing. Rebalancing is the process of bringing the portfolio back toward its intended allocation; it is not a forecast of where markets will go next.

Example: a 60/40 portfolio drifts to 70/30

Target allocation
60% equity / 40% debt
→
Market drift
70% equity / 30% debt
→
Rebalance
Return toward 60/40

Illustrative example: Actual portfolio weights and required transactions depend on market values and cash flows.

1. Build the allocation plan

Use: Time horizon, Tolerance for temporary declines, and Trade-off between expected returns and short-term declines.

2. Set a rebalancing policy

Rebalance when allocation deviates by approximately ±5%, once every 6 or 12 months. Choose and document a consistent review frequency.

3. Check before trading

Consider tax, transaction costs, liquidity, cash flows and whether the goal or risk profile has changed. New contributions may help restore balance without selling.

E · Entry & Exit Plan

Decide how to enter—and how to protect a goal

Separate, deployment of a lump sum from regular SIP investing, then outline a de-risking plan for goals that are approaching.

Lump-sum entry

Debt allocation: deploy immediately, subject to product suitability and liquidity needs.

Equity allocation: use a combination of staggered investments and one-time investment rather than making the decision emotionally.

The proportion and schedule for staged equity deployment should be set for the investors.

Regular SIP entry

Equity exposure: allocate across the five styles: Quality, Value, Growth, Mid & Small Cap, and Global/Momentum.

Debt exposure: split the debt SIP across Fund A 40%, Fund B 30% and Fund C 30%.

These fund labels are placeholders; they are not specific fund recommendations.

Exit / de-risking plan

As a goal approaches, the right action depends on whether the goal is critical or the timeline can be delayed. The aim is to reduce the chance that a sudden market fall disrupts an essential expense.

Goal protection timeline

3 years to goal
Critical · Cannot be delayed

Move existing equity into selected DAAFs. Direct new contributions to conservative, high-quality, shorter-duration debt funds.

2 years to goal
Secure the required amount

Start a 2-year STP from DAAF into suitable conservative, shorter-duration debt funds. Keep new contributions in suitable debt funds.

3 years · Flexible goal
Goal can shift by 1–2 years

Move equity into selected DAAFs and direct incremental SIPs into selected DAAFs, subject to the investor's risk capacity.

DAAFs are market-linked and do not guarantee the goal amount. Product selection and transition timing must be personalised to the goal, risk profile, liquidity needs and tax position.

5. Pre-investment checklist

Protect the base Safety reserves, insurance and near-term liabilities have been reviewed.
Write the triggers Bubble and Crisis Plan indicators, tranche amounts and pause conditions are documented before markets move.
Check suitability Equity/debt mix, fund types, liquidity, taxes and risk capacity match the investor and goal.
Set review dates Choose a periodic review and a rebalancing drift band, consistent with approximate ±5% guideline.
